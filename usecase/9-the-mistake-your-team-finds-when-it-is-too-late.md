And yet at least twice a month, sometimes thrice a Bill of Lading went out with an error in it.

Not a big error. Never anything obviously wrong. A transposed digit in a seal number. A consignee name with one word different from the Letter of Credit. ## The SI to Draft B/L Gap — Where Every Error Is Born

The process looks simple on paper.

Shipper sends Shipping Instructions. The documentation team prepares Draft B/L. Shipper approves. Original B/L issued.

Four steps. Clean. Logical.

What actually happens inside step two is where the problem lives.

A team member receives the SI by email, PDF, WhatsApp, sometimes a photograph of a handwritten note. They open the B/L template. And they begin typing.

Shipper name. Consignee. Notify party. Cargo description. HS Code. Container number. Seal number. Gross weight. Measurement. Marks and numbers. Port of Loading. Port of Discharge. Vessel. Voyage number. Freight terms.

**Fifteen to twenty fields. Manually entered. Under time pressure. Across eight to twelve active shipments simultaneously.**

Every single field is a copy-paste opportunity for a human error. And humans, doing the same repetitive task at speed, will always eventually make one.

## It was dreadful

The specific feeling in Levi's documentation team was not stress about workload.It was **dreadful**.

Not the panic of a crisis happening now. The quieter, heavier dread of knowing a crisis was coming  and not knowing when, or which shipment, or which field would be the one that caused it.

The team worked carefully. They double-checked. They had a colleague review the Draft B/L before sending. And errors still got through because two humans checking the same manually typed data against a source document, under deadline pressure, will miss the same things.

*"My team is not careless. They are doing everything right. And we still get B/L errors every month. That tells me the problem is not the people. The problem is the process."*

Levi understood the root cause clearly. What he didn't have was a way to fix it that didn't involve hiring more people to check the work of the people already checking.

## **What CEO Actually Wanted**

* **Zero errors** that survived to the Original B/L stage  
* Documentation team spending their time on **judgment and exceptions** not data entry  
* The confidence to issue an Original B/L knowing that **every field had been cross-checked** before it left the office

He wasn't asking for perfection from his team. He was asking for a system that didn't rely on human perfection to function.

## **What Pagsun Built**

Pagsun's diagnosis was precise: the error lives in the gap between the SI arriving and the Draft B/L being typed. Eliminate the human in that gap and you eliminate the error.

The AI Employee was built to own that gap entirely.

**Phase 1 : SI Channel Mapping** Identify every format and channel through which SIs arrive email, PDF, WhatsApp, portal. Build extraction for each.

**Phase 2 : AI Employee Design** Build the extraction, auto-population, and three-source discrepancy check Booking Note, SI, and LC cross-reference. 

**Phase 3 : Draft B/L Workflow Integration** Embed the flagged discrepancy report into the draft approval process so every shipper approval is deliberate, not reflexive.

**Phase 4 : Amendment Tracking** Track every flagged discrepancy, every correction made, and every clean B/L issued building a quality record over time.

Six weeks after deployment, a shipment came in for a regular shipper, a buyer paying under a Letter of Credit from a bank in Dubai.

The shipper submitted their SI as usual. The AI Employee extracted it, populated the Draft B/L, and ran the three-source check.

It flagged one discrepancy.

The LC required the goods description to read *"Basmati Rice, Non-Parboiled, Long Grain"* in exactly that sequence, with those commas. The SI had submitted it as *"Long Grain Non-Parboiled Basmati Rice."* Same words. Different order.

To a human checker reading quickly, it would have looked identical. The meaning is the same. But a bank processing an LC does not read for meaning it reads for exact match. That one transposition would have resulted in a documentary discrepancy, a bank rejection, and a shipper unable to collect payment until an amended B/L was issued and re-presented.

The AI Employee caught it in seconds. The draft was corrected before it left the office. The Original B/L was issued clean. The shipper collected payment on time.

Levi's documentation manager sent one message to Pagsun that evening:

*"This would have been a problem. We would not have caught this one."*
