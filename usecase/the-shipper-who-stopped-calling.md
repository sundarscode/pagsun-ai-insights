John had been shipping through the same NVOCC for two years.

Good relationship. Fair rates. No major problems. The kind of shipper every NVOCC founder wants on their books is consistent volumes, regular lanes, and pays on time.

Then quietly, without a single complaint, without a single confrontational conversation he stopped calling.

Not all at once. First he booked one shipment elsewhere. Then two. Then his volumes with the NVOCC dropped by sixty percent over four months. Then stopped entirely.

When the NVOCC founder, Raj, finally noticed and called to ask what had happened John was polite. Almost apologetic.

*"Nothing went wrong. Your team is good. Your rates are fine. It is just that whenever I need to move cargo I need a decision quickly. By the time your quote comes, I have already booked elsewhere."*

That sentence landed like a quiet verdict.

The NVOCC had not lost John to a better price. It had not lost him to a service failure. It had not lost him to a competitor with a stronger relationship.

It had lost him to **a faster reply.**

## The Quote That Arrives Too Late

A shipper's decision window is short.

When John needed to move a container, his planning horizon was immediate. He had a cargo ready, a delivery deadline at the destination, and a vessel cut-off approaching. He sent rate enquiries to three NVOCCs simultaneously not to play them against each other, but because he had learned that response times were unpredictable and he could not afford to wait.

Whoever responded first with a clear, complete rate and a confirmed sailing got the booking.

Not necessarily the lowest rate. Not necessarily the longest relationship. The first clear answer.

Inside Raj's office, the enquiry from John would arrive by email, or WhatsApp, or both and enter a queue. The operations team was handling active shipments. The rate calculation required checking the current tariff, confirming space availability with the carrier, calculating the full charge breakdown including surcharges, and formatting a quote email that looked professional.

On a good day this took two to three hours. On a busy day when multiple shipments were at cut-off, when the carrier's system was slow, when a team member was on a call it took until the next morning.

By then, John had his booking confirmation from someone else.

**The NVOCC never knew they had lost the inquiry.** There was no rejection email. No *"we went with another provider."* Just silence. The quote was sent. No reply came. Raj's team assumed John had decided not to ship that week.

He had shipped. Just not with them.

## **The Invisible Damage**

In this case study sits on both sides of the relationship and they are both quiet.

**The shipper is** not angry. It is not dissatisfaction. It is the mild, practical disappointment of a person who wanted to do business with you, gave you the opportunity, and moved on when the window closed. John did not leave because he wanted to. He left because waiting was not an option his supply chain allowed.

**The founder** is different and in some ways harder. It is the particular sting of discovering you have been losing business without knowing it. Not through failure, not through incompetence, but through a structural gap in your process that has been silently draining revenue for months or years.

*"The worst part is I cannot tell you how many Johns there have been. I only know about this one because he answered when I called. The rest I will never know."*

---

## **What Raj Actually Wanted**

* A rate response that reached the shipper **within minutes** not hours, not the next morning  
* Every quote **complete** ocean freight, surcharges, THC, documentation fee so the shipper had a decision-ready number, not a starting point for more back-and-forth  
* **Sailing options** presented clearly so the shipper could choose without having to ask follow-up questions  
* A way to **convert a warm enquiry to a confirmed booking** without requiring a human to be available at the exact moment the shipper was ready to decide

He did not want to replace his team's commercial relationships. He wanted to make sure a shipper who was ready to book right now could do so regardless of whether his team was available right now.

## **What Pagsun Built**

The core of the solution was simple in principle and precise in execution.

When a rate enquiry arrived from any channel the AI Employee responded. Not in hours. In minutes.

---

**Phase 1 : Enquiry Channel Audit** Map every channel through which rate enquiries arrive email, WhatsApp, web form, phone. Build the AI Employee response layer for each.

**Phase 2 : Rate Card Automation** Connect the AI Employee to the current tariff and carrier space confirmation. Build the full charge breakdown generation for each trade lane.

**Phase 3 : Booking Confirmation Workflow** Build the one-click confirmation link and the operations team approval notification so human review is fast without being a bottleneck.

**Phase 4 : Rate-to-Invoice Link** Connect the confirmed rate card to the Final Invoice generation so every booking confirmed through the AI Employee carries zero invoice surprise downstream.

---

### **What Stayed Human**

The AI Employee handled the response, the rate card, and the booking request. The humans handled everything that required judgment.

If the shipper had a special cargo hazardous, oversized, temperature-controlled the AI Employee flagged it and routed it to the operations team immediately. No automated rate for cargo that required human assessment.

If the shipper wanted to negotiate if they came back asking whether the rate had any flexibility that conversation happened between the shipper and a human. The AI Employee had created the opening; the relationship was still owned by the team.

And every booking confirmed through the AI Employee generated the same Rate Card that fed directly into the Final Invoice connecting back to the fix built in Case Study 4\. No rate-invoice gap. No surprises three weeks later.

## Three weeks after deployment

Raj called John. Not to ask for business back. To tell him something had changed and invite him to send an enquiry.

John sent a test enquiry at 7:43 PM deliberately, after office hours, to see what would happen.

At 7:51 PM he received the full rate card with three sailing options and a booking confirmation link.

He booked the next morning. He called Raj directly after:

*"Eight minutes. After office hours. I did not expect that."*

Within sixty days John had moved four shipments through Raj's NVOCC. Not because the rates had changed. Not because the relationship had been repaired through charm or persuasion.

Because the clock problem had been solved.

## **AI Employee Performance**

**Within 90 days of deployment:**

* **Quote response time** dropped from an average of 4.5 hours to under 12 minutes  
* **Enquiry-to-booking conversion rate** increased from 36% to 61%  
* **After-hours enquiries** previously lost entirely now captured and responded to automatically  
* **Two lapsed shippers** returned after receiving a response to a test enquiry they had sent without expecting an answer  
* **Zero additional headcount** the improvement came entirely from the AI Employee, not from hiring more operations staff

Raj did not win back every shipper he had silently lost. Some had formed new relationships elsewhere and were not coming back regardless of response time.

But he stopped losing new ones. And he recovered enough lapsed ones to make the question he asked himself  *"how many Johns have there been?"* feel less like a wound and more like a lesson.

*"The rate was never the problem. The relationship was never the problem. We were just not there when they were ready. Now we are always there. That is the only thing that changed."*
